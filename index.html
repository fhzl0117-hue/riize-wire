SEOUL — SEPTEMBER 2026

Three years in,
six voices, still rising.

RIIZE spent the first half of 2026 closing out Riizing Loud, the world tour behind debut album Odyssey — a run that ended with the fastest Tokyo Dome entry ever by a K-pop boy group, 120,000 fans across three nights in February, before wrapping in Seoul in March. By June the group was back with second mini-album II, led by "Do your dance" — a track whose lyrics carry a solo credit for Haru, daughter of Epik High's Tablo, after she won a blind songwriting screening at SM. A third Japanese single, "Sunburst," followed on August 26, and the group's sold-out 3rd anniversary fanmeeting lands in Incheon on September 12–13. This page is a working build of how RIIZE Wire tracks all of it for English-speaking BRIIZE — translated, sourced, and converted to whatever timezone you're reading from.

A note on the lineup: RIIZE debuted in September 2023 as a seven-member group. Member Seunghan left in October 2024 and now performs independently as XngHan; RIIZE has continued as six members (Shotaro, Eunseok, Sungchan, Wonbin, Sohee, Anton) since. The fandom name was also changed from an earlier name to "BRIIZE" after fans raised concerns about it.

Desk 02 · Review

Revisiting the catalogue, track by track.

Text only, always — no reposted photos or video frames on this desk.

3.8 / 5EDITOR'S STAMP
"A six-track burst of confidence that trades Odyssey's widescreen ambition for something looser and more danceable — exactly what a title called 'Do your dance' should sound like."

II (2026): RIIZE's second Korean mini-album, and the group's first comeback since the Riizing Loud tour wrapped. Where Odyssey leaned into a wide sonic palette across ten tracks, II is tighter and more direct — six songs built around hip-hop-inflected pop. "Do your dance" is the most straightforwardly fun title track the group has released, and it's carried in part by an unusually personal credit: Haru Lee, daughter of Epik High's Tablo, wrote the Korean lyrics after joining SM's in-house songwriting team through a blind screening process.

Coverage has centered as much on that songwriting story and the tour records that preceded it — the fastest-ever K-pop boy group Tokyo Dome run — as on the record's own chart performance, which arrived too recently for a full picture at publication.
